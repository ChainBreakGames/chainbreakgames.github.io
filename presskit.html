Undervalued is an antique shop management game for 1–4 players about the slow, unglamorous art of knowing what things are worth — and convincing someone else to agree.

Every customer is a negotiation waiting to happen. Price too high and they walk. Price too low and you've just funded someone else's weekend. Sell in-store. Move inventory online. Flip the same lamp three times if that's what it takes. The job rewards knowledge, patience, and a tolerance for the printer jamming at the worst possible moment.

There's also the matter of your mood. Working surrounded by other people's unwanted belongings, in a shop you didn't exactly dream of running, takes a quiet toll. Managing that drain is part of the job — the options available to you range from reasonable to deeply questionable.

Undervalued is designed around co-op: split the workload, argue over valuations, and decide who has to deal with the customer who's been deliberating over a ceramic bowl for forty minutes. The game supports up to 4 players, with experimental support for up to 99 — for those who feel antique retail was always meant to be a spectator sport.

- Antique shop management with in-store sales and an online marketplace
- Price negotiation system built on knowledge-based progression
- Mood system with consequences
- A city to explore beyond the shop floor
- Co-op for 1–4 players (experimental support up to 99)
- A PC in-game to waste time on
- Paperballs
